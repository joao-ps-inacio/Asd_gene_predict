    How the ranking was made

    Two classifiers from the thesis score each gene. Both learned from the same labels: SFARI Gene as of January 2024 (category 1 and syndromic genes as positives) and the low-risk genes of Krishnan et al. (2016) as negatives.

    • Protein model. The gene's canonical protein sequence, embedded with the ProtT5 protein language model, then XGBoost.
    • Network model. The gene's position in the STRING protein-interaction network, embedded with DeepWalk, then logistic regression.

    Training genes were scored by cross-validation, so their scores come from a model that never saw them. All other genes were scored by a model trained on every labelled gene.

    Did it work? SFARI has added genes since the models were trained. The chart shows where those genes sat among the genes that had no label in 2024.

    Read with care. This is a research ranking, not a diagnostic tool. Long genes collect more rare variants and get discovered more often, which explains part of the result. Most added genes are SFARI category 3, the weakest evidence level. Network embeddings partly reflect how well studied a gene already is.
